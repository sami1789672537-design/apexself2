Introduction
WHAT THIS COURSE IS

Looksmaxxing is the practice of maximising your physical appearance through intentional, consistent effort. At its core, it's about understanding what affects how you look — and systematically improving those things within your control.

This isn't about becoming someone you're not. It's about removing friction, building healthy habits, and presenting the best version of yourself with confidence. Think of it as applied self-improvement, rooted in biology and aesthetics.

"You can't choose your genetics, but you can choose how close to your genetic ceiling you get."

This course covers six core domains: facial analysis and self-assessment, skincare, body and physique, hair and style, lifestyle optimisation, and social presence. Together they form a complete system.

HOW TO USE THIS COURSE

Work through each module in order. The foundation modules (1–4) should be started simultaneously in your first two weeks. Advanced modules (5–6) layer in from week 3 onwards. Use the 12-Week Action Checklist (included as a bonus) to stay on track.

  • Weeks 1–2: Complete Modules 1 and 2. Begin Module 3 training framework.
  • Weeks 3–6: Establish skincare routine, training, and grooming habits from Modules 3 and 4.
  • Weeks 7–10: Introduce lifestyle optimisation from Module 5.
  • Weeks 11–12: Focus on presence and personal brand from Module 6.
Pro tip: Take a full set of photos before you start — front, side, and back. Progress is hard to see day-to-day but dramatic over 12 weeks when you compare.
Module 01
FOUNDATIONS & FACIAL ANALYSIS
01
Foundations & Facial Analysis
Understand your starting point before you build
Before you can improve, you need to assess. Most people skip this step and waste time fixing things that aren't their weak points. This module gives you the framework to see yourself objectively and build a personalised improvement roadmap.

LESSON 1.1 — WHAT LOOKSMAXXING IS (AND ISN'T)

Looksmaxxing is evidence-based, intentional self-improvement focused on appearance. It is not:

  • An obsession with perfection or chasing unachievable standards
  • Dangerous or harmful practices — we focus only on safe, sustainable methods
  • A substitute for self-acceptance — confidence and looks complement each other

The realistic goal: close the gap between your current presentation and your natural genetic ceiling. For most people, this gap is significant, and closing even half of it is transformative.

"Soft-maxxing" (skincare, fitness, grooming, sleep, style) can realistically add 1–3 points on any subjective attractiveness scale, which translates to dramatically different life outcomes.

LESSON 1.2 — FACIAL HARMONY & THE GOLDEN RATIO

Facial attractiveness research consistently identifies a few structural principles:

Exercise: Take a photo in even, natural daylight with a neutral expression. Use the "thirds" method — draw horizontal lines at your hairline, browline, bottom of nose, and chin. Assess which section is visually short or long. This tells you where to focus.

LESSON 1.3 — OBJECTIVE SELF-ASSESSMENT

Humans are notoriously bad at assessing their own looks — we either over-inflate or over-criticise. Here's a protocol for honest assessment:

  1. The 3-photo method: Take front, 45-degree, and direct profile shots in natural light. This is what others actually see.
  2. The 5 pillars: Rate yourself honestly on skin, hair, physique, grooming, and style — each out of 10. Your lowest score is your biggest opportunity.
  3. The feedback question: Ask 2–3 trusted friends what one thing they'd change about your appearance. Patterns reveal genuine blind spots.
  4. Reference collection: Identify 3–5 people whose style and aesthetic you want to emulate. Note what specifically you're drawn to — it's rarely genetics; it's usually presentation.

Module 02
SKINCARE MASTERY
02
Skincare Mastery
Clear, healthy skin is the single highest-ROI change you can make
Good skin doesn't require expensive products or complicated routines. It requires consistency with the right basics. This module gives you a complete skincare system — from a simple starter routine to advanced actives for real results.

LESSON 2.1 — KNOW YOUR SKIN TYPE

There are five skin types. Knowing yours determines which products you use.

Skin TypeSignsKey Focus
NormalBalanced, few issuesMaintain with basics
OilyShiny by midday, large pores, breakout-proneLightweight, non-comedogenic products
DryTight feeling, flaky patches, dullRich moisturiser, gentle cleansing
CombinationOily T-zone, dry cheeksZone-specific treatment
SensitiveReacts easily, redness, stingingFragrance-free, minimal actives
Quick test: Wash your face, wait one hour without applying anything, then check. Shiny overall = oily. Tight/flaky = dry. Mix of both = combination.

LESSON 2.2 — THE CORE ROUTINE (AM & PM)

The "Core Four" — all you actually need to start:

  1. Cleanser — removes dirt, oil, pollutants. Use twice daily. Gel for oily skin, cream for dry.
  2. Moisturiser — hydrates and strengthens the skin barrier. Non-negotiable, even oily skin types.
  3. SPF (AM only) — SPF 30–50 every morning. UV damage is the number one cause of premature ageing. This single step matters more than any other product.
  4. Active treatment (PM only) — retinol, niacinamide, or vitamin C depending on your goal (see Lesson 2.3).
"The best skincare routine is one you actually do every day. Simple and consistent beats complex and irregular."

Budget-friendly picks (UK): CeraVe Hydrating Cleanser (£10), CeraVe Moisturising Cream (£12), La Roche-Posay Anthelios SPF50 (£18), The Ordinary Retinol 0.2% (£5).

LESSON 2.3 — ACTIVES: THE REAL GAME CHANGERS

Don't combine: retinol + AHA/BHA in the same routine (too irritating). Vitamin C + niacinamide is fine. Keep it simple when starting.

LESSON 2.4 — FIXING ACNE, HYPERPIGMENTATION & SCARRING

For active acne: Start with a BHA cleanser (salicylic acid 2%) and niacinamide serum. Add benzoyl peroxide (2.5% — less irritating than 5%) for stubborn spots. Avoid picking — it always makes scarring worse.

For hyperpigmentation (dark spots): Vitamin C serum (morning) + SPF is your baseline. Add tranexamic acid or alpha-arbutin for targeted treatment. Results take 6–12 weeks of consistency.

For acne scarring:

  • Textured/indented scars: Retinol long-term; consider professional microneedling for significant scarring.
  • Flat dark marks (post-inflammatory hyperpigmentation): Treat as hyperpigmentation above.
  • Red/pink marks: These typically fade naturally over 3–6 months. Niacinamide can accelerate this.
Module 03
BODY & PHYSIQUE
03
Body & Physique
Train for aesthetics, not just performance
Your physique is the foundation everything else sits on. The goal here isn't to be a bodybuilder — it's to achieve proportions that look naturally athletic and powerful with clothes on. This module gives you the framework to get there efficiently.

LESSON 3.1 — THE AESTHETICS-FIRST TRAINING FRAMEWORK

Aesthetic training prioritises certain muscle groups over others. The most impactful groups for visual impact:

Muscle GroupAesthetic PurposePriority

Upper chestFills shirts, projects confidenceHighest

Train 4 days per week with an upper/lower split. Target each muscle group 2x per week with 12–20 working sets total per muscle group weekly.

LESSON 3.2 — OPTIMAL BODY COMPOSITION

For most men, the aesthetic sweet spot is 10–15% body fat with meaningful muscle mass. At this range:

  • Facial features become more defined (most visible at 12% and below)
  • Jawline and cheekbone definition emerge
  • Clothes fit dramatically better
  • Testosterone levels tend to be optimal
"The most important looksmaxxing transformation most people can make is losing body fat. Everything else — skincare, grooming, style — matters more when your face is lean."

Fat loss basics: Create a 300–500 calorie daily deficit through a combination of diet and training. Track protein intake (aim for 0.8–1g per lb of bodyweight). Prioritise sleep (see Module 5). Fat loss is primarily a dietary challenge; training preserves muscle and improves body shape.

LESSON 3.3 — NUTRITION FOR AESTHETICS

Keep it simple. The science consistently supports:

  • Protein first: 150–200g per day for most people. Keeps you full, preserves muscle, supports skin health.
  • Whole foods base: 80% of your diet from whole, minimally processed foods. Highly processed diets worsen skin quality and energy.
  • Hydration: 2.5–3.5 litres of water daily. Dehydration is immediately visible in skin texture, eye puffiness, and energy.
  • Limit alcohol: Alcohol disrupts sleep, increases cortisol, causes water retention (particularly visible in the face), and impairs recovery. Even 3–4 drinks a week has measurable cosmetic effects.
  • Omega-3s: From oily fish or supplementation. Directly improves skin quality, reduces inflammation.

LESSON 3.4 — POSTURE & MEWING

Posture may be the most overlooked element of attractiveness. Good posture:

  • Makes you appear taller and more confident
  • Projects status and self-assurance
  • Reduces neck and jaw tension
  • Improves how clothes sit on your body

Daily fixes: dead hangs (30–60 seconds, twice daily), face-pulls (3 sets, 3x per week), strengthen your glutes and core, consciously check your posture when sitting at screens.

Mewing: The practice of tongue-posture correction — resting your tongue against the roof of your mouth, lips together, teeth slightly apart. The evidence for dramatic facial restructuring in adults is limited, but the posture improvements and jaw relaxation benefits are real. Practise it habitually rather than obsessing over results.

Module 04
HAIR, GROOMING & STYLE
04
Hair, Grooming & Style
Immediate impact — the fastest return on any investment in your appearance
A good haircut, well-maintained beard, and clothes that fit properly can transform your appearance within 24 hours. These are the fastest wins available to you — and they compound with everything else in this course.

LESSON 4.1 — FINDING YOUR HAIRCUT

The right haircut for your face shape:

Communication tip: Show your barber 2–3 reference photos of styles you like. Say "I want something like this, adapted for my face shape." A good barber will guide you. Go to an actual barber shop, not a unisex salon.

LESSON 4.2 — BEARD GROOMING

Whether to grow a beard depends on your face. Beards work best when they add definition to an undefined jaw or balance a weak chin. If you already have a strong jawline, stubble (3–7 days) often reads better than a full beard.

Growing phase (weeks 1–4): Resist shaping. Let it grow in fully before you make decisions. Many people give up during the "patchy" phase at 2–3 weeks — push through.

Maintenance:

  • Define the cheek line (remove stray hairs above your natural line)
  • Define the neckline (two finger-widths above your Adam's apple)
  • Use beard oil or balm daily for softness and appearance
  • Shape with scissors and a comb for longer beards
The most common mistake: Setting the neckline too high. The neckline should follow the natural curve of your jaw, not your jawline itself — it's lower than most people think.

LESSON 4.3 — FRAGRANCE: YOUR INVISIBLE WEAPON

Scent creates powerful, immediate impressions. Build a small collection of 2–3 fragrances for different occasions.

Warm/orientalWinter, intimate occasionsTom Ford Noir, JPG Ultramale

Application: Apply to pulse points — wrists, neck, chest. Don't rub. 2–4 sprays maximum. The goal is a pleasant presence, not a cloud you walk through. Stronger concentration = longer lasting: EDP > EDT > EDC.

Budget option: Check Dossier or Armaf for quality dupes at a fraction of designer prices. Nobody knows what your fragrance costs by smelling it.

LESSON 4.4 — FASHION BY BODY TYPE & BUDGET

The three rules that matter more than anything:

  1. Fit is everything. A £30 shirt that fits perfectly beats a £300 shirt that doesn't. Get things tailored if needed — a tailor is a game-changer.
  2. Colour palette matters. Build around neutrals (navy, white, grey, black, olive, beige). Add one or two accent colours you personally suit. Avoid clashing patterns when you're starting out.
  3. Quality over quantity. 10 versatile, well-fitting pieces beat a wardrobe of 40 items that don't work together.

Core wardrobe essentials (UK sources):

  • 2–3 well-fitting white/navy t-shirts (UNIQLO, M&S)
  • 2 pairs dark slim/straight jeans (ASOS, Levi's 511)
  • 1 smart chino (COS, UNIQLO)
  • 1 versatile navy or grey jacket (Zara, & Other Stories sale)
  • Clean, simple trainers — white leather works with almost everything (Nike AF1, Adidas Stan Smith)
  • 1 quality watch (any clean, simple face — doesn't need to be expensive)
Module 05
LIFESTYLE & HEALTH OPTIMISATION
05
Lifestyle & Health Optimisation
Your biology is a looksmaxxing tool
Most people focus only on the external — products, clothes, training. But your hormones, sleep quality, stress levels, and health markers affect your appearance more directly than almost anything else. This module covers the biological side of looksmaxxing.

LESSON 5.1 — SLEEP AS A LOOKSMAXXING TOOL

Sleep is when your body repairs skin, regulates hormones, and reduces inflammation. Poor sleep is visible on your face within 24 hours — puffiness, dark circles, dull skin, and reduced jaw definition from water retention.

Optimise your sleep:

  • Target 7.5–9 hours of quality sleep (track with any wearable or free app)
  • Consistent wake time — even on weekends. This is the most powerful circadian lever.
  • Room temperature 16–18°C for optimal sleep quality
  • No screens 60 minutes before bed, or use blue light glasses
  • Sleep on a silk pillowcase — reduces friction on skin and hair
  • Slightly elevate your head — reduces morning puffiness and fluid retention in the face
"One week of 6-hour sleep nights measurably reduces facial attractiveness ratings. One week of returning to 8 hours reverses it."

LESSON 5.3 — SUN EXPOSURE, TANNING & SKIN HEALTH

A light, natural tan improves the appearance of most skin tones. But unprotected UV exposure is the single biggest driver of premature skin ageing — wrinkles, spots, and sagging.

The framework:

  • Daily SPF 30–50 (non-negotiable — see Module 2)
  • 15–20 minutes of unprotected sun on arms/legs for vitamin D synthesis (avoid face)
  • For a tan: use SPF 30 and simply spend time outdoors. Build gradually.
  • Self-tanner or gradual tanning moisturiser: great for a subtle glow without UV damage
  • Never use sunbeds. UV damage from sunbeds is aggressive and cumulative.

LESSON 5.4 — STRESS, CORTISOL & YOUR FACE

Chronic stress elevates cortisol, which directly:

  • Breaks down collagen (skin becomes thinner and less elastic)
  • Triggers acne and inflammation
  • Disrupts sleep and testosterone production
  • Causes fat gain, particularly around the face and abdomen

Stress management isn't soft advice — it's biochemistry. Build daily stress management into your routine: 10–20 minutes of low-intensity exercise (walks count), breathwork (4-7-8 breathing or box breathing), reducing caffeine after noon, and limiting news/social media consumption which creates chronic low-level anxiety.

Module 06
PRESENCE, CONFIDENCE & SOCIAL SKILLS
06
Presence, Confidence & Social Skills
How you carry yourself changes how the world sees you
Physical appearance matters — but so does how you occupy space, how you communicate, and the energy you bring to interactions. The most attractive version of you is a combination of how you look and how you carry yourself. This module ties everything together.

LESSON 6.1 — BODY LANGUAGE THAT COMMUNICATES STATUS

High-status body language signals:

  • Slow movement: Urgency signals anxiety. Deliberate, unhurried movement reads as confidence.
  • Space: Take up appropriate space — don't compress yourself. Wide stance, relaxed arms at sides.
  • Head position: Chin slightly up, not tucked. Looking up vs down changes face perception significantly.
  • Eye contact: Hold eye contact 60–70% of the time when listening, a little less when speaking. Look away to the side, not down.
  • Stillness: Fidgeting, touching your face, and nervous movement are signals of anxiety. Practise stillness.
Daily practice: Record yourself having a conversation on your phone. Watch it back without sound. The patterns you see are what others see. Most people are shocked by what they discover.

LESSON 6.2 — VOCAL TONE & COMMUNICATION

Your voice is as important as your face. A strong, clear, resonant voice dramatically changes how you're perceived.

Common issues and fixes:

  • Upspeak (rising inflection at end of sentences): Sounds uncertain. Consciously end declarative statements with a flat or falling tone.
  • Speaking too fast: Slowing your speech rate 20% immediately sounds more confident.
  • Vocal fry: That creaky sound on end of phrases. Improve breath support and hydration.
  • Volume: Many people speak too quietly. Project from your diaphragm, not your throat.

Daily exercise: Read aloud for 10 minutes per day. Vary your pace, emphasis, and tone intentionally. This builds awareness and control.

LESSON 6.3 — LOOKING GREAT IN PHOTOS

In the age of social media, knowing how to photograph well is a skill worth mastering.

  • Lighting: Soft, even natural light (facing a window or outdoors in shade) beats harsh direct sunlight. Golden hour (1 hour after sunrise or before sunset) is flattering to almost everyone.
  • Angle: Camera slightly above eye level, looking up slightly reduces double chin and broadens jaw. Never below eye level for portrait shots.
  • Expression: A relaxed, slight smirk (not a forced grin) reads best. The "squinch" — slightly squinting while smiling — is research-backed to read as confident and attractive.
  • Posture: Lean slightly forward, not back. Push your chin slightly forward and down (yes, this feels odd but looks natural in photos). Brings jaw forward and reduces perceived double chin.
  • Background: Clean, uncluttered backgrounds. Or environmental context that tells a story about your life. A blurred background draws focus to your face.

LESSON 6.4 — BUILDING YOUR PERSONAL BRAND ONLINE

Your online presence is part of your appearance in the modern world. A consistent, well-presented profile signals status, confidence, and social proof.

Core principles:

  • Profile photo: High quality, well-lit, smiling (slight). This is your first impression — invest in it.
  • Consistency: Same username across platforms. Same profile photo or visual style.
  • Show, don't tell: Don't say you're interesting — post photos that demonstrate your lifestyle, hobbies, and values.
  • Less is more: A clean, curated feed with 10 great posts beats 100 average ones. Remove anything that doesn't represent the person you're building toward.
"Your social media is a portfolio of your life. Curate it like one."
